**Targets.** FY15–FY17: target 41%, threshold 40%. From FY18: target 64%, threshold 60%.

**Reading the series.**
- Use the corrected values for FY15–FY16. The originally reported values (as low as 32.74%) were wrong, and FRTIB corrected "the algorithm used to calculate this metric" (minutes, Nov 2016).
- Retention was flat at about 64–65% from FY18 Q2 to FY20 Q3. It then rose to about 70–73% (FY21 Q3–FY22 Q2), after the TSP Modernization Act's flexible withdrawal options took effect on 15 Sep 2019. It has since eased to about 67–69% (FY24–FY26).
- Minutes of Apr 2022 (https://www.frtib.gov/meeting_minutes/2022/2022Apr.pdf): "The one-year post-separation statistics show nearly seven out of ten participants retain a balance with the TSP, likely due to the expanded flexibility of the withdrawal program."
- The metric counts anyone with a balance above $0. A one-time partial withdrawal or an installment stream still counts as "retained".

## 2. TSP: what the 2012 separator cohort had done by the end of 2013 (pre-modernization)

**Source.** FRTIB memorandum to the Board, 7 Jul 2015, "withdrawal flexibility" (MM-2015Jul-Att7, in https://minutes.frtib.gov/meeting_minutes/2015/2015Jul.zip). It is also summarized by FedSmith, 28 Jul 2015: https://www.fedsmith.com/2015/07/28/expanding-withdrawal-options-in-the-tsp/

The memo "examined the group of participants who separated in 2012 and assessed what they had done with their TSP balances by the end of 2013":

| Outcome | Share |
|---|---|
| Took no action | 49% |
| One-time partial withdrawal only | 2% |
| Cashout (vested balance under $200, paid out automatically) | 8% |
| Full withdrawal | 41% |

The 41% full withdrawal breaks down as:
- 37% lump sum, split into 11% transferred to another institution and 26% taken as cash
- 4% monthly payment stream
- 1% annuity

Further detail from the memo:
- Cash lump sums averaged $18,830. Transferred lump sums averaged $126,557. So 75% of lump-sum dollars went to other institutions.
- "In 2013 alone, separated participants transferred $9 billion out of the TSP." Of these, 27% cited a desire for withdrawal flexibility and 36% a major expense or life event.
- This is also cited in House Report 115-343 (TSP Modernization Act): https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRPT-115hrpt343/html/CRPT-115hrpt343-pt1.htm
- Implied share fully exiting within about 1–2 years: 41% full withdrawal + 8% cashout = **49% closed**. The "45%" figure in the brief was **not found** in this memo. It may be a different cut, such as lump sum plus cashout = 45%. **[UNVERIFIED]**
- Ages are pooled; no age split was given.

## 5. Surveys and academic studies of what leavers do (not recordkeeper data)

**HRS, ages 50+.** Banerjee (EBRI), statement to the ERISA Advisory Council, 17 Jun 2014, "Disposition of DC Accounts: Who Rolls Over into an IRA? Who Leaves Money in the Plan and Who Withdraws Cash?" URL: https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/EBSA/about-ebsa/about-us/erisa-advisory-council/2014-facilitating-lifetime-plan-participation-banerjee-06-17.pdf (also EBRI Notes, May 2014).
- Data: HRS 2008 and 2010 pooled, n = 1,380. Question: "When you retired/left the employer, what did you do with the plan?" Survey data, not recordkeeper data. One cross-section, no trend.
- **Still in the labor force after leaving:** 34.8% left the money in the plan, 27.0% rolled to an IRA, 18.4% cashed out.
- **Retired from last job:** 42.8% "receiving benefits now", 21.5% rolled to an IRA, 17.2% left in the plan, 15.1% withdrew the money. Small categories: annuity about 1.2% and installments about 1.9%. **[small-category mapping read from chart; main values in text/figure]**
- **By balance:** cash-out 46.1% for balances under $5k vs 9.6% for $100k and above. IRA rollover 13.7% vs 47.0%. Left in plan about 31–32% below $100k vs 25.4% at $100k and above.

**SIPP, ages 21+, lump-sum recipients only.** Copeland, EBRI Notes, Nov 2013 (Vol. 34, No. 11), "Lump-Sum Distributions at Job Change, Distributions Through 2012". URL: https://www.shrm.org/content/dam/en/shrm/topics-tools/news/benefits-compensation/EBRI_Notes_11_Nov-13_LSDs-WBS.pdf (EBRI page: https://www.ebri.org/content/lump-sum-distributions-at-job-change-4153)
- Data: 2008 SIPP Topical Module 11. Census survey data, so not the EBRI recordkeeper database.
- It covers only people who took a lump sum. It does not measure leave-in-plan.
- Share of recipients putting the **entire** distribution into tax-qualified savings: 19.3% (survey through 1993), 35.4% (1998), 43.4% (2003), 44.3% (2006), 45.2% (2012).
- Share using **any part** for tax-qualified savings, by year received: 20.5% (before 1980), 30.5% (1980–86), 50.1% (1987–93), 51.5% (1994–98), 51.9% (1999–2003), 52.2% (2004–06), 47.9% (2007–09), 45.9% (2010–12).
- Entire amount to tax-qualified savings, by age at distribution: 32.4% (30 or under), 44.4% (31–40), 51.5% (41–50), 54.8% (51–60), 55.9% (61–64), 41.5% (65+).
- Earlier SIPP rounds: CRS RL30496 (Purcell, "Pension Issues: Lump-Sum Distributions and Retirement Income Security", updated 3 Aug 2005), https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RL30496.html. Per the search summary, 45% rolled the entire most recent distribution into an IRA or plan, which was 70% of lump-sum dollars. **[figures from search summary; not opened]**

**Tax administrative data.** Goodman, Mackie, Mortenson and Schramm, "Leakage from Retirement Savings Accounts in the United States", *National Tax Journal* 74(3), 2021, pp. 689–719. URL: https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/715819
- Data: IRS panel, 2003–2015, people age 50 or under.
- Distributions equal 22% of contributions.
- Job separation raises the probability of leakage by more than 200%.
- A trade-press summary says leakage is about 26% in the year of separation, 17% in year 2 and 11% in year 3. **[secondary-source numbers, UNVERIFIED]**
- Does not cover retirement-age leavers.

**GAO-15-73** (Nov 2014), *401(k) Plans: Greater Protections Needed for Forced Transfers and Inactive Accounts*. URL: https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-15-73
- Data: Form 8955-SSA data held by SSA.
- 2004–2013: separating employees left more than 16 million accounts of $5,000 or less in workplace plans, worth $8.5 billion in total. Quoted in Munnell, CRR/MarketWatch, 12 Jan 2015: https://crr.bc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/Greater-Protections-for-Inactive-401k-Accounts-–-Center-for-Retirement-Research.pdf

- No rate and no age split.

**PSCA plan-sponsor survey (63rd Annual Survey).** Summarized at https://www.psca.org/news/psca-news/2021/10/qotw-retaining-participants/ and in trade press.
- About three-quarters of sponsors do not actively try to retain ex-participant assets, though most allow them to stay.
- 77.8% set a minimum balance to stay: 20.6% at $1,000 and 57.2% at $5,000.
- **No percentage of terminated participants who stay was found** in PSCA material. **[not verified in primary PDF]**

**State and local 457 or DC plans.** The documents found (Sacramento County, Washoe County) give point-in-time counts of terminated participants with balances. Examples: Sacramento, Q2 2024, 4,315 terminated or inactive vs 10,027 active; Washoe, Dec 2021, 1,019 terminated with a balance. These are recordkeeper-prepared plan reviews, so outside scope, and they give no retention rate or trend. No public-plan retention time series was found.

**Not done.** The Survey of Consumer Finances asks about DC accounts from past jobs, which could give a 1989–2022 trend of families holding money in a former employer's plan. No published tabulation was found; it would need microdata work.

## 6. Bottom line for a time series
1. **Best series (government, administrative data): TSP 1-year FERS post-separation retention.**
   - FY15–FY16: about 57–61%
   - FY18–FY20 (before modernization): about 64–65%
   - FY21–FY22: about 70–73%
   - FY24–FY26: about 67–69%
   - All ages pooled, FERS only.
2. **TSP stock measure.** The separated share of accounts rose steadily: about 21% (end 2011) → 24% (2013) → 29% (2018) → 35% (2025). Separated accounts grew from about 0.96 million to 2.5 million.
3. **Private-plan analogue.** In Form 5500 data, inactive 401(k) accounts rose from 19.0% (2014) to 22.4% (2023) on a consistent basis. Inactive includes retirees and beneficiaries.
4. **Older leavers (HRS 2008/10).** Only about 17% of people retiring from their last job "left it in the plan". About 43% were "receiving benefits", which may include installments from the plan. A further 21.5% rolled to an IRA and 15% cashed out. Among older job changers who kept working, 35% left it in the plan.
