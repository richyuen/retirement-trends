# Candidate new areas for the report (2026-10-05)

Richard asked what other retirement trends the project could cover, such as health care spending, and which trends people often cite. Nothing below has been researched yet; this is a menu. "Reachable" was tested from this environment on 2026-10-05 (HTTP 200 on the site's home page). The figures in "what people cite" are the commonly quoted numbers from memory, not yet checked against the sources.

Already covered and not repeated here: withdrawals, RMDs, rollovers, stay-in-plan, DC plan laws (S12), income sources and the CE budget mix incl. health care's 12-14% share (S13), longevity (S14).

## Recommended first

### 1. Health care costs in retirement (beyond the CE budget share)
- What people cite: a 65-year-old couple needs about $300k+ for health care in retirement (Fidelity, recordkeeper estimate); Medicare Part B premiums growing faster than Social Security COLAs, eating a rising share of the benefit; out-of-pocket costs heavily skewed (top 10% of spenders).
- Best public sources: MEPS (AHRQ) out-of-pocket spending by age, 1996-2023; CMS Medicare Trustees Report and Part B premium history; CMS National Health Expenditure by age; CE already in hand; KFF analyses of Medicare OOP.
- Reachable: meps.ahrq.gov, cms.gov, data.cms.gov, kff.org, govinfo.gov all yes.
- Ties in: extends S13 (health share flat but premiums rising inside it) and S14 (longer lives = longer cost horizon). A public alternative to the Fidelity number, in percentages of income, fits Richard's preference for non-recordkeeper sources.

### 2. Long-term care risk and cost
- What people cite: about 7 in 10 people turning 65 will need some long-term care (ASPE/HHS); a private nursing-home room costs over $100k/yr (Genworth, private survey); Medicaid pays the majority of nursing-home care; private LTC insurance sales collapsed.
- Best public sources: ASPE lifetime-risk briefs; CMS NHE (nursing home, home health spending by payer); MEPS; CMS nursing-home data.
- Reachable: aspe.hhs.gov, cms.gov yes. Genworth is private and not tested.
- Ties in: the biggest tail risk to the drawdown rates in S4-S10 and to S14 longevity.

### 3. Working longer and later retirement
- What people cite: labor force participation at 65+ roughly doubled since the mid-1990s (about 12% to about 19-20%); average retirement age creeping up; fewer claim Social Security at 62.
- Best public sources: BLS CPS series via download.bls.gov (works; bls.gov pages 403); CPS ASEC microdata already in hand; Social Security claiming data is on ssa.gov (blocked), but the Trustees Report on govinfo has some claiming tables.
- Ties in: S13 earnings share rising 17% to 27-29%; scf_6064 found more 60-64 holders working (70% to 77%), which lowers withdrawals. Directly explains part of the withdrawal trends.

### 4. Social Security solvency
- What people cite: the combined trust funds run out in the mid-2030s, after which about 80% of scheduled benefits are payable (roughly a 20% cut).
- Best public source: 2026 Trustees Report (already downloaded via govinfo by the longevity thread).
- Ties in: S13 (Social Security is 32% of 65+ income, larger for lower-income retirees). Cheap to add because the source is in hand.

## Also worth considering

### 5. Retirement readiness and coverage gaps
- What people cite: about half of households at risk of not maintaining their living standard (Boston College CRR National Retirement Risk Index); about half of working-age households have no retirement account; median balances near retirement are modest.
- Sources: SCF (in hand, all waves 1989-2022); crr.bc.edu reachable; EBRI Retirement Confidence Survey (ebri.org reachable).
- Ties in: S9-S12. Risk: the "retirement crisis" debate is contested (e.g., Biggs vs CRR), so this needs both sides.

### 6. Debt and housing wealth among older households
- What people cite: more people carrying mortgages into their 70s; older households' share of total debt rising; home equity is the largest asset for most retirees.
- Sources: SCF (in hand); New York Fed Household Debt and Credit report (newyorkfed.org reachable); Census homeownership by age; CFPB on reverse mortgages (consumerfinance.gov reachable).
- Ties in: S13 (housing is 36% of the 65+ budget) and possible pressure to withdraw more.

### 7. Elderly poverty: official vs supplemental measure
- What people cite: official 65+ poverty about 10%, but the Supplemental Poverty Measure is several points higher, mainly because of out-of-pocket medical costs.
- Sources: Census (reachable), CPS ASEC microdata in hand.
- Ties in: pairs naturally with #1 (health costs push retirees into poverty).

### 8. Decline of DB pensions
- What people cite: share of private workers in a DB plan fell from about 38% (1980) to about 15%.
- Sources: DOL Private Pension Plan Bulletin (dol.gov 403); Form 5500 data partly in hand (dc_stay/); PBGC (reachable); BLS NCS via download.bls.gov.
- Ties in: S13 DB share 19% to 13%. Partly covered already, so lower priority.

### 9. Lifetime income and annuities
- What people cite: very few retirees annuitize ("annuity puzzle"); SECURE Act made in-plan annuities easier.
- Sources: mostly private (LIMRA) or academic; SCF has annuity ownership. Weaker public data.
- Ties in: S12 laws and S14 longevity risk.
