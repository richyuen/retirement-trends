# IRA withdrawals among retirement-age owners: incidence, amounts, rates and trends (ICI, EBRI, IRS SOI)

Scope: United States, traditional and Roth IRAs, 2000 through the latest data available on 3 October 2026 (ICI household survey: tax year 2024; ICI IRA Investor Database: 2023; IRS SOI: tax year 2023; EBRI IRA Database: 2017).

Conventions used throughout
- "TY" = tax year in which the withdrawal was taken. The ICI IRA Owners Survey is fielded in May/June of year t and asks about withdrawals in tax year t-1; every ICI survey figure below is labelled by the tax year of the withdrawal (survey year minus one).
- Three categories are kept apart wherever the source allows: (1) partial withdrawals, (2) full cashouts (account depleted or closed), (3) rollovers/transfers/Roth conversions. None of the three sources publishes a clean "full cashout" count; what each source includes or excludes is stated in the section "How often are IRAs fully liquidated or closed" and in "Gaps and caveats".
- "computed" marks a number I calculated from published cells; the inputs are shown or are in the adjacent columns. Nothing is interpolated or estimated.
- How documents were opened: ICI and IRS Excel files were opened directly (cell values read from the .xls/.xlsx files); ICI, IRS and EBRI PDFs were read as text. EBRI Issue Briefs are behind a member login, so EBRI's public summary pages, press releases, an infographic, two public EBRI Notes PDFs and trade-press reports were used for those (flagged where used).

## ICI IRA Owners Survey ("The Role of IRAs in US Households' Saving for Retirement"): share of traditional IRA-owning households taking a withdrawal, who they are, how the amount is set, how much, and what the money is used for

### Takeaway
Between 15% and 33% of traditional IRA-owning households report a withdrawal in a given tax year: 18-22% in TY2006-TY2014 (except 15% in the 2009 RMD-suspension year), 25-27% in TY2015-TY2019, 23% in the 2020 waiver year, and 29%, 31%, 31%, 33% in TY2021-TY2024 (the series high). Withdrawals are overwhelmingly an RMD phenomenon: 81-90% of withdrawing households are retired (since TY2015), 81-84% of RMD-age households withdraw versus 5-8% of households under 59, and 70-77% of withdrawing households say the amount was set by the RMD rule (48% in TY2009, 61% in TY2020); the median withdrawal is $14,000-$15,000 (TY2022-TY2024), a median 5-6% of the balance.

### Inferences
- The step up from about 20-22% (TY2010-TY2014) to 25-27% (TY2015-TY2019) coincides exactly with the 2016 switch from a telephone to an online panel, so part of that step is a survey-mode effect rather than behaviour; the within-mode trend is flat in the telephone years and rising in the online years (25% in TY2015 to 33% in TY2024).
- The rising incidence since TY2015 is consistent with ageing of the owner population: RMD-age households rose from 16-17% of traditional IRA-owning households (TY2006-TY2013) to 23-24% (TY2021-TY2024) and make up about 60% of withdrawing households.
- Because 44-47% of retired withdrawing households reinvest or save at least part of the withdrawal, and about 90% of those are RMD-driven, a large share of "withdrawals" is not consumption; it is forced distribution that is re-saved in taxable accounts.
- The mean withdrawal has roughly doubled since TY2014-TY2016 ($11,500-$20,100) to $29,000-$32,800 in TY2021-TY2024 while the median percentage of balance stayed at 5-6%, which points to larger balances rather than faster drawdown.

### Gaps
- Mean and median withdrawal amounts are only published for all withdrawing households; there is no published split by age or by RMD vs. discretionary withdrawals in this survey.
- The uses-of-money series is missing TY2014 (the 2015 edition's sentence was not captured) and TY2007 is only given in words.
- For TY2007 and TY2008 the allocation of two middle bands of the amount distribution ($5,000-$9,999 and $10,000-$14,999) could not be verified from the figure text (17 and 13 for TY2007; 16 and 15 for TY2008, order uncertain), so the full distributions for those two years are not listed.
- The survey excludes households that closed their traditional IRAs, so it contains no information on full cashouts.
- The mean/median for TY2009, TY2012, TY2015 and TY2018-TY2022 were read from the figure text of the PDFs by an automated extraction; the values pass internal consistency checks (amount bands sum to 100) but were not re-verified against a data file.

## ICI IRA Investor Database ("The IRA Investor Profile: Traditional IRA Investors' Activity" and "Roth IRA Investors' Activity"): withdrawal rates by age and year, amounts, percentage of balance, RMD-only vs. more than RMD

### Takeaway
In ICI's administrative database (6.6 million traditional IRA investors in 2023), 17.6% of traditional IRA investors took a withdrawal in 2007, 13.9% in 2009 (RMD suspension), 25.1% in 2019, 20.3% in 2020 (CARES Act waiver) and 23.1% in 2023; among investors at or above RMD age the rate is 78-85% in normal years, 46.9% in 2009 and 59.6% in 2020, while investors aged 60 to RMD age stay near 18-22% and those under 60 at 5-8.5%. Among RMD-age investors who withdraw, 52-58% take exactly the Uniform Lifetime RMD and 33-39% take more (2011-2023); the median withdrawal for the RMD-age group is about 5-6% of the prior year-end balance regardless of balance size. Roth IRA investors withdraw at 3-4% a year, only about 6% even at age 70 or older.

### Inferences
- Investors between age 70 and the (new) RMD age now behave like investors in their sixties: 25.7% of those aged 70-71 withdrew in 2022 and 27.7% of those aged 70-72 in 2023, versus 75.0% of those aged 70-74 in 2018. The "RMD age or older" rate itself (81-85%) is unchanged by the higher starting age.
- The share taking more than the RMD has been stable at 33-39% of RMD-age withdrawers for more than a decade, including in the 2020 waiver year (35.7%); what changed in 2020 was that the "exactly RMD" group shrank (57.2% in 2018 to 43.6%) and the "less than RMD" group grew (4.1% to 17.9%).
- The flat 5-6% median withdrawal across all balance bands at RMD age (versus 68-90% for small balances in the sixties and at ages 70-72) indicates that pre-RMD withdrawals from small accounts are mostly near-total liquidations of what was there a year earlier, while RMD-age withdrawals are formula-driven partial withdrawals.
- The database rate (23.1% in 2023) is lower than the ICI household survey (31% for TY2023) because the unit is one investor at one provider rather than a household across all its IRAs.

### Gaps
- Detailed age snapshots and RMD comparisons exist only for the report years (2007, 2008, 2011-2016, 2018, 2020, 2022, 2023); there is no published snapshot for 2009, 2010, 2017, 2019 or 2021 beyond the four-column time series.
- The 2021 row of Figure A.11 does not state which age split was used (60-69/70+ as in 2020, or 60-71/72+ as in 2022).

## EBRI IRA Database (Craig Copeland; data years 2010-2017): share of owners withdrawing by age and IRA type, amounts, withdrawal rates, RMD-only vs. more, and longitudinal results

### Takeaway
EBRI's administrative database gives the same picture for 2011-2017: about 25-27% of traditional IRA owners and 3-4% of Roth IRA owners take a withdrawal in a year; for traditional owners the rate jumps from 27% at ages 65-70 to 84-86% at ages 71 and older (2013), the median withdrawal rate at ages 71-79 is 4.1% of the balance, and only about a quarter of owners aged 71 or older (22.7-26.9% per year) take more than their RMD; over six years 57% of them took only the RMD every year. The last EBRI cross-section is for 2017 (published September 2020); nothing newer was found.

### Inferences
- EBRI (25-27% of traditional owners), the ICI database (22-24%) and the ICI survey (25-27% of households, TY2015-TY2019) agree on the level for the mid-2010s; EBRI's 71-79 rate (83.6% in 2013, 85.4% in 2016) is close to ICI's 70+ rate (80-82%).
- EBRI and ICI measure "more than RMD" on different bases, so the two series should not be spliced: EBRI reports about 23-30% of IRA owners aged 71 or older (wording: "of the IRA owners ages 71 or older"), ICI reports 33-39% of RMD-age investors who took a withdrawal. Putting ICI's 2023 figure on an all-investor base gives 0.817 x 35.6% = 29.1% of all investors aged 73 or older (computed), which is in EBRI's range. Both say a clear majority of RMD-age owners take the RMD and no more.
- The drop from "almost 24%" (2014-2016) to "just over 21%" (2017) coincides with the database shrinking from 19.1 million to 9.2 million individuals, so it is probably a composition change in the contributing providers rather than a behavioural change.

### Gaps
- EBRI Issue Briefs (2014, 2015, 2016, 2017 Updates; Longitudinal 2010-2014/2015/2016; Issue Briefs 477 and 503) are behind a member login; only their public summaries were available, so age-by-type tables, average/median withdrawal amounts and withdrawal rates are missing for 2014-2017.
- No EBRI IRA Database results were found for data years after 2017, so EBRI has nothing on the 2020 waiver or on the SECURE Act age changes; EBRI's database starts in 2010, so it also has nothing on the 2009 suspension.
- The 2011 and 2012 figures are per account, the 2013-2017 figures per individual; they are not strictly comparable.
- The exact base of EBRI's "in excess of RMD" percentages in the annual Updates (all owners 71+ or only those with a withdrawal) is not stated in the public summaries.
