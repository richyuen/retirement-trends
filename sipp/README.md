# SIPP microdata: IRA and 401(k) withdrawals by age, reference years 2020-2024 (direct from Census)

Built 2026-10-04 from the Census Bureau's public-use SIPP files (www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/sipp/data/datasets/YYYY/). Nothing comes from IPUMS. SIPP file year t covers calendar (reference) year t-1. SIPP 2025 (ref year 2024) was already out, so it is included as well.
Re-run: `python3 sipp/scripts/extract.py /tmp/sipp/raw && python3 sipp/scripts/tabulate.py /tmp/sipp/reps && python3 sipp/scripts/validate.py` (downloads about 7 GB; about 25 minutes, mostly downloading; tabulation about 1 minute).

## What is here
- `scripts/extract.py [RAW_DIR] [YEARS]` downloads `puYYYY_csv.zip` and `rwYYYY_csv.zip` for 2018-2025 into RAW_DIR (default `/tmp/sipp/raw`, outside the shared folder). It reads the pipe-delimited csv inside each zip in chunks, keeping only the needed columns, and keeps the December (MONTHCODE=12) record for each person. It also sums the monthly retirement-income recode TRETINCAMT over the year. Each zip is deleted after extraction.
  - `data/sipp_persons_dec.parquet` (7 MB): 367,611 person rows, all ages, from 8 files (31-64k persons per file).
  - The December replicate weights (REPWGT1-240, persons 15+) go to `/tmp/sipp/reps/rwYYYY_dec.parquet`. They total 340 MB, so they are kept out of the shared folder. Re-run extract.py to rebuild them.
- `scripts/tabulate.py` -> `output/sipp_estimates_long.csv` (6,900 cells with SEs), `output/sipp_headline_wide.csv` (person level, year-end holders, ref years as columns, with `se_YYYY` columns), and `output/sipp_rmd_single_age.csv` (IRA incidence by single year of age, 68-76).
- `scripts/validate.py` -> `output/sipp_validation_census_wealth.csv` (comparison with Census's published wealth tables) and `output/sipp_vs_irs_ira.csv` (comparison with IRS SOI IRA Table 4, using the project's `data/irs_derived.json`).

Long-table keys: `ref_year`, `sipp_year`, `unit` (person/household), `account` (ira/dc/any; any_incl_db for the 2017-2019 bridge item), `holders` (see below), `age_band`, `measure`, `estimate`, `se`, `n` (unweighted cases).

## Source variables (exact meaning, from the Census data dictionaries and instrument specifications)
| Variable | Meaning | Files |
|---|---|---|
| `EOWN_IRAKEO` / `EOWN_THR401` | Owned any IRA or Keogh / any 401k, 403b, 503b or Thrift Savings Plan account **at any time** in the reference year (person level, the owner's own accounts) | 2018-2025 |
| `TIRAKEOVAL` / `TTHR401VAL` | Value of those accounts **"as of the last day of the reference period"** (Dec 31 of ref year). Topcoded: topcoded cases carry the mean of the topcoded values, and `TIRAKEO_MED`/`TTH401_MED` give their median | 2018-2025 |
| `EIRA_INC_YN`, `TIRA_INC_AMT` | "At any time in [year], did ... receive any income or withdrawals from [his/her] IRA or Keogh account(s)?" and the annual amount ("Between January 1st and the end of December, how much income did ... receive") | **2021-2025 only** |
| `ETHR_INC_YN`, `TTHR_INC_AMT` | The same for 401k/403b/503b/thrift plans. The question adds ", excluding any lump sum payments reported earlier" when a pension/retirement-plan lump sum was reported in the lump-sum section | 2021-2025 |
| `ELMPTYP1YN`, `TLMPAMT`, `EROLLOVR1`, `TROLLAMT` | Lump sum from a pension/retirement plan; total lump sums (this includes severance); whether any of it was rolled over to an IRA or other plan; the rollover amount | 2018-2024 (2025 renamed) |
| `ERET_LUMPSUM` | "Receipt of any lump sum or regular distribution payments from a retirement plan" (1 lump sum, 2 regular, 3 both, 4 none). Asked of IRA, 401k or DB-pension holders aged 59+ in December. Covers IRAs, DC plans **and DB pensions** together | 2018-2020 only |
| `TDRAW_AMT` | Amount of lump-sum or regular distributions from an IRA, Keogh, 401k, thrift, DB or cash-balance plan "that was not reported in ERETTYP1YN-ERETTYP8YN" | 2019-2020 only |
| `EEVERET` | Ever retired from a job or business | all |
| `TAGE_EHC` (MONTHCODE 12) | Age in December of the reference year (top-coded at 90) | all |
| `WPFINWGT`, `REPWGT1-240` | Final person weight (December record) and the calendar-year replicate weights; REPWGT0 equals WPFINWGT (max difference 0.004) | all |
| `A*` status flags | 0 not in universe, 1 as reported, 2-8 imputed (hot deck, logical, model, cold deck, range) | all |

What the questions do **not** separate: RMDs vs other withdrawals, Roth vs traditional IRAs, partial withdrawals vs full cash-outs, and IRA-to-IRA rollovers or Roth conversions. The IRA question says only "income or withdrawals", so a respondent could count a rollover or conversion. A 401(k) lump sum is asked in the lump-sum section, with an explicit rollover follow-up, and is kept out of `ETHR_INC_*`. There are no withdrawal months: the items are annual. SIPP's monthly detail does not reach account withdrawals. `TRETINCAMT` spreads all retirement income by month, including pensions, so it is not used.

## Definitions
- **Unit.** The main unit is the person, because SIPP asks each owner about their own accounts. The household version uses SSUID+ERESIDENCEID in December: a household holds or withdraws if any member does, dollars are summed across members, and age and weights come from the household reference person (ERELRPE 1/2).
- **Age** is the age in December of the reference year, which is close to the IRS's end-of-tax-year age. SCF ages, by contrast, are measured at interview, about a year later.
- **Holders.** In `yearend` (the headline), the person owns the account type and has a balance > 0 on Dec 31 of year t, which matches the project definition of incidence ("per 100 year-end holders"). `anytime` also counts owners whose year-end balance was zero. `yearend_retired` restricts to EEVERET=1, the consistent universe for the 2020 comparison (see caveat 1). `linked_prior_yearend` covers people interviewed in both SIPP t and SIPP t+1 of the same panel who were year-end holders in t-1 and still report owning the account type at some point in t.
- **Incidence** = withdrawers per 100 holders.
- **Dollar-weighted rate, all holders** = sum of withdrawals in t / sum of balances, over holders. The headline uses the **Dec 31, t** balance (end of the same year), because that is what every respondent has. The `linked_prior_yearend` rows use the **Dec 31, t-1** balance reported in the previous SIPP file, which is the project's definition (withdrawals in t / balance at end of t-1). They rest on about 2-3.4k linked cases per year and are weighted with the year-t weight, without an attrition adjustment.
- **Withdrawers only**: the dollar-weighted rate (sum of W / sum of B among withdrawers) and the weighted median of each withdrawer's own W/B.
- **Topcode sensitivity** (`*_topcode_median_*`). About 3% of withdrawers 60+ have a topcoded amount. In the public file these cases are set to the mean of the topcoded values: IRA $341k, $360k, $170k, $386k, and in SIPP 2025 $1.09M. The sensitivity replaces them with the published median of the topcoded values (`TIRA_INC_MED`, `TTHR_INC_MED`). This lowers the dollar-weighted rates, mostly in 2024.
- **Combined (any)**: IRA + DC withdrawals / IRA + DC balances; a person is a withdrawer if they withdrew from either account type.
- **SEs** come from Fay's BRR with the 240 calendar-year replicate weights: variance = 4/240 x sum of (theta_r - theta)^2. The same method is used for medians. Household SEs use the reference person's replicate weights.

## Validation
1. **Census published wealth tables match** (`sipp_validation_census_wealth.csv`). Household ownership of retirement accounts by age of householder, from our extract, against Census "Wealth, Asset Ownership, & Debt of Households: 2022" (SIPP 2023) and ": 2023" (SIPP 2024), Table 2 (percent holding) and Table 1 (conditional medians). All 36 ownership cells are within 0.3 points. For 2022: any account 60.1 vs 60.3, IRA 31.9 vs 31.9, 401k/thrift 49.6 vs 49.9; householders 65+: 51.5/37.4/30.3 vs 51.6/37.4/30.5. Medians mostly match exactly (2022: $75,000 all, $150,000 at 55-64). The largest gap is $3,120 (2023 IRA 65+, $126,880 vs $130,000), within the gap between Census's interpolated medians and our plain ones.
2. **Record check.** The 2024 file's unweighted counts and means match Census's `pu2024_validate.xlsx`: EIRA_INC_YN N=6,803 persons (81,636 person-months / 12), mean 1.739; TIRAKEOVAL mean $254,979.
3. **Against IRS SOI (IRA only)** (`sipp_vs_irs_ira.csv`). The age profile and the 2020/2023 dips line up, but SIPP levels are lower. In 2021-2023, SIPP counts 76-83% of IRS's IRA holders 60+ and 83-89% of their balances, but only 55-63% of IRS withdrawers and 47-68% of IRS withdrawal dollars. Incidence at 75+ is 74-83 in SIPP vs 94-101 in IRS (IRS counts can exceed 100 because withdrawers whose accounts are empty at year end still count). Same-year rates for 70+: SIPP 3.9-4.4% vs IRS 5.1-6.3%. Part of the gap is definitional: IRS distributions include Roth conversions and some rollovers, and IRS counts every taxpayer with a 1099-R. Part is survey under-reporting of withdrawals, as with CPS.

## Findings (person level, year-end holders, ref years 2021-2023 unless noted; 2024 = SIPP 2025)
1. **Incidence follows the RMD schedule.** Per 100 IRA holders, incidence is 6-7 at 55-59, 11-14 at 60-64, 19-25 at 65-69, 50-56 at 70-74, 74-80 at 75-79 and 77-83 at 80+. For 401(k)/thrift/DC holders the figures are 5-6, 10-15, 19-22, 40-54, 58-76 and 64-79. For either account type: 60+ 40-43, 65+ 51-55.
2. **The RMD-age moves show up clearly at single years of age** (`sipp_rmd_single_age.csv`; SE about 4 points per cell). IRA incidence at age 72 is 64-67 in 2020-2022 (that cohort's first RMD year under the SECURE Act's age 72) and falls to **37 in 2023 and 30 in 2024**, when SECURE 2.0 moved the first RMD to 73. Age 73 is 73-76 in 2021-2023 and 67 in 2024 (57 in the 2020 waiver year). Age 71 falls from 45 (2020; that cohort turned 70½ in 2019 under the old rule) to 28-36 (2021-2024). In the 70-74 band, IRA incidence goes from 55.5-55.8 (2021-2022) to 49.8 (2023) and 48.1 (2024), matching IRS (69.1 -> 61.7).
3. **The 2020 RMD waiver is visible at 75+ but shallower than in IRS data.** Among retired IRA holders, incidence at 75-79 was 62.4 in 2020 vs 73.5, 80.2 and 79.1 in 2021-2023, and at 80+ 63.0 vs 77.3, 83.1 and 81.7. That is a 10-20 point dip, against 20-25 points in IRS. The all-holder rate dipped too: any account, 75-79: 3.3% vs 3.8-4.8%; 80+: 4.4% vs 5.4-5.5%. The withdrawers-only median rate did not change (80+: 6.6% vs 6.0-6.9%). As in the SCF's 2009 waiver year, people stopped withdrawing, but those who withdrew took the usual share.
4. **All-holder (dollar-weighted) rates are small.** For any account: 60+ 2.3-2.7% (1.8-2.5% with topcode medians), 60-69 1.2-1.7%, 70+ 3.7-4.1%, 80+ 5.4-5.5%. For IRAs: 60+ 2.6-3.0%, 70-74 2.6-3.2%, 80+ 6.2-6.8%. DC rates are lower (60+ 1.3-2.5%). Using the prior-year-end balance (linked panel) gives about the same: any account 60+ 2.3-2.9%, 70+ 3.5-4.6%.
5. **Withdrawers-only rates are about 5-6%, stable across age and years.** For any account, 60+: dollar-weighted 5.4-5.7% (4.4-5.6% with topcode medians); median of individual rates 5.1-5.6%. By age the median is 7.7-9.0 at 60-64, 5.7 at 65-69, 4.1-4.7 at 70-74 (a little above the first-year RMD: 1/27.4 = 3.6% at 72, 1/26.5 = 3.8% at 73), 4.5-5.1 at 75-79 and 6.0-6.9 at 80+. They rise with age the way the Uniform Lifetime Table does (4.9% at 80, 6.3% at 85), which suggests most withdrawers at 70+ take about the RMD.
6. **Ref year 2024 (SIPP 2025) dollar-weighted rates jump**: any account, 60+ all-holder 3.7%, withdrawers 8.2%. The jump comes from the topcode: the SIPP 2025 topcode mean for IRA withdrawals is $1.09M, against a median of $306k for the topcoded cases. With topcode medians the figures are 2.3% and 5.2%, in line with earlier years, and the medians of individual rates (5.5%) did not move. Treat the 2024 dollar-weighted figures as fragile.
7. **Few withdrawers empty their accounts.** Among owners 60+ who withdrew, 1.1-1.9% (IRA) and 3.7-6.2% (DC) ended the year with a zero balance. These are full cash-outs, as far as SIPP can show them.
8. **Households** (`unit=household`). Incidence is higher than for persons, because two spouses get two chances: households with a 60+ reference person, 44-47 per 100 (2021-2023); 70+, 67-71. Rates are similar: all-holder 60+ 2.3-2.7%; withdrawers-only median 4.9-5.3%.

## Comparison with SCF and Poterba-Venti-Wise (PVW)
| Measure, ages 60+ unless noted | SIPP 2021-2023 (this part) | SCF 2003-2021 (scf/) | PVW, SIPP 1997-2010 |
|---|---|---|---|
| All-holder dollar rate | 2.3-2.7% (persons), 2.3-2.7% (households) | 2.2-3.1% | |
| ... before 70 / 70+ | 60-69: 1.2-1.7%; 70+: 3.7-4.1% (households 1.3-1.9 / 3.6-3.9) | 60-64 about 1%, 80+ 5-7% | before 70 about 1.9%; after 70 about 5.8% (all PRA households) |
| Withdrawers-only, dollar-weighted | 5.4-5.7% (topcode medians: 4.4-5.6%) | 5.2-6.9% | |
| Withdrawers-only, median of own rates | 5.1-5.6% | 5.7-7.5% | |
| IRA incidence 70-74 / 80+ | 50-56 / 77-83 per 100 IRA holders | 61-80 / 80-92 per 100 households (all accounts) | |

SIPP's all-holder rates fall inside the SCF range, and its withdrawers-only rates are at the low end of the SCF's (SIPP's ages are about a year younger, and SIPP is person level). Compared with PVW, SIPP gives about the same rate before 70 but a lower rate at 70+: 3.7-4.1% vs about 5.8%. PVW divides by household PRA balances under a different design and covers 1997-2010, when the RMD age was 70½ (so more of the 70-74 band was drawing), so the gap is probably partly definitional.

## Caveats
1. **SIPP 2021 (ref 2020) asked the withdrawal items only of owners classed as retired** (instrument `NOW_RET=1`, which equals `EEVERET=1` on the file: 5,948 of 10,513 IRA owners are blank). From SIPP 2022 on, all owners are asked. The 2020 `yearend` and `anytime` cells therefore cover retired holders only, and at 55-69 they are not comparable with later years (2020 holder counts at 60-64 are 2.6M vs about 6.5M). Use `holders=yearend_retired` for 2020-vs-later comparisons. At 75+ nearly everyone is retired, so the effect is small.
2. **No account-specific withdrawal items before SIPP 2021.** For ref years 2017-2019, the only item is `ERET_LUMPSUM`, which pools IRAs, DC plans and DB pensions and is asked only at age 59+ (`bridge_any_plan_dist_per100`: 70-74 about 67, 80+ about 78). It is not comparable with the 2020+ series and is shown only for completeness. Its amount (`TDRAW_AMT`) excludes distributions reported as regular retirement income, so its implied rate (about 1%) is not meaningful.
3. Survey under-reporting relative to IRS (validation 3). Withdrawal items are imputed for 8-11% of holders 60+ (`wd_item_imputed_pct`).
4. Dollar-weighted rates are sensitive to the topcode (finding 6). Report the median of individual rates alongside them, or the `*_topcode_median_*` bounds.
5. RMDs, Roth accounts, rollovers and conversions are not identified. Roth IRA holders, who have no RMD, are in the IRA denominator, as in IRS and SCF.
6. Cell sizes: about 730-1,130 IRA holders at 70-74 and 480-780 at 80+ per year. SEs for incidence are 1.5-2.2 points per band, and for single years of age about 4 points.
7. Persons not in sample in December (deaths, movers out) are excluded, because the December weight is used. Holders who died during the year are therefore missed.
