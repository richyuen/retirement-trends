2024 RCS FACT SHEET #1

RETIREMENT CONFIDENCE
One in four Americans feel very confident about their ability to have enough money to
live comfortably throughout their retirement years. Workers who say debt is a problem
are, not surprisingly, less confident, while those who have a retirement plan are
remarkably more confident.

Confidence in Having a Comfortable Retirement
The 2024 Retirement Confidence Survey (RCS) finds just over two-thirds of American
workers (68 percent) feel confident in their ability to have enough money to live
comfortably throughout their retirement, with 21 percent feeling very confident (Figure
1). Neither of these levels are statistically significantly different from the levels in 2023.

The level of confidence expressed by those already in retirement is higher than for those
yet to retire. Seventy-four percent of retirees report feeling either very or somewhat
confident about having enough money to live comfortably throughout their retirement
years, which is unchanged from 2023. Just over one-quarter of retirees feel very
confident (28 percent) in 2024, also unchanged from 2023 (Figure 2).

Retirement confidence continues to be strongly related to retirement plan
participation, whether in a defined contribution (DC) plan, defined benefit (DB) plan, or
individual retirement account (IRA). Workers reporting they or their spouse have money
in a DC plan or IRA or have benefits in a DB plan from a current or previous employer
are more than twice as likely as those without any of these plans to be at least
somewhat confident (77 percent with a plan vs. 34 percent without a plan) (Figure 3).

Confidence in Other Financial Aspects of Retirement
Three in four workers (76 percent) report feeling very or somewhat confident about
being able to afford basic expenses in retirement, including 32 percent who feel very
confident. Workers’ confidence in their ability to afford basic expenses is higher than
the confidence they report regarding their ability to pay for medical expenses in
retirement. One in three workers (34 percent) are not too or not at all confident they will
have enough money for medical expenses in retirement. A similar share is not too or not
at all confident that they are doing a good job in preparing financially for retirement
(32 percent) (Figure 4).

Retirees, who are already in that life stage, express higher levels of confidence than
workers in having enough money to take care of expenses (basic and medical) during
retirement. More than four out of five retirees (82 percent) feel at least somewhat
confident in their ability to afford basic expenses throughout their retirement years.
Seventy-eight percent are very or somewhat confident about having enough money to
cover medical expenses, and 72 percent feel they did a good job preparing financially
for retirement (Figure 4).

Influence of Debt on Confidence
Workers are more likely to say that debt is a problem for them than retirees. Sixty
percent of workers say debt is a problem for them, while 40 percent say debt is not a
problem. In contrast, just 36 percent of retirees say that debt is a problem for them.
Workers are nearly twice as likely as retirees to say that debt is a major problem (18
percent vs. 10 percent) (Figure 5).
The RCS has consistently found a relationship between debt levels and retirement
confidence. In 2024, 34 percent of workers with a major debt problem say they are very
or somewhat confident about having enough money to live comfortably in retirement,
